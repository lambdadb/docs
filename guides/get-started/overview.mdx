LambdaDB is **the vector lake for AI memory**. Store, search, and version AI knowledge and memory, from small experiments to production, without managing always-on servers.

Documents, vectors, and indexes live in durable object storage. [On-demand compute](/guides/get-started/architecture) handles indexing and retrieval, with [usage-based pricing](/guides/costs/understanding-costs).

## Store your knowledge and memory

Organize source documents, embeddings, and metadata in [collections](/guides/collections/collection-overview). Choose [index types](/guides/collections/index-types) for the fields your application needs to search or filter. Supply your own vectors, or use [native embeddings](/guides/collections/native-embeddings) to generate document and query vectors from text with provider credentials managed by LambdaDB.

## Search with the right signals

Find relevant documents using [full-text](/guides/search/query-string), [dense vector](/guides/search/vector), or [sparse vector](/guides/search/sparse-vector) search. Combine retrieval paths with [hybrid search](/guides/search/hybrid), then optionally apply [native reranking](/guides/search/native-reranking) to evaluate the retrieved text against your question.

Use [filters](/guides/search/boolean) to narrow matches and [keyword facets](/guides/search/facets) to show counts alongside supported lexical queries. The [search overview](/guides/search/search-overview) covers query options and links to each search method's detailed guide.

## Version and recover your data

Use [branches, tags, and aliases](/guides/data-versioning/branches-tags-aliases) to isolate changes, pin datasets for evaluation, and switch application reads between versions. Collections retain committed snapshots for 30 days by default, with [configurable retention](/guides/collections/create-a-collection#snapshot-retention). Create a [point-in-time branch](/guides/data-versioning/branches-tags-aliases#point-in-time-branches) from retained history to inspect or recover an earlier state.
